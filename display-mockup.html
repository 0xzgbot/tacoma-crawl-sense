The display

Buy the Waveshare 7inch HDMI LCD (C). It is a 7-inch IPS panel, 1024×600, which is the size the program opens. HDMI carries the picture. A micro-USB plug on the same board powers it and would carry touch, which this screen does not use. The Pi stays under the dash. This pod is only the panel.

Waveshare lists the module at 164.9 × 107.0 × 8 mm and the lit area at 154.2 × 85.9 mm. Brightness is about 300 cd/m², so the printed pod wants a short hood or the noon sun on a Tacoma dash will wash it out. Their wiki has a dimension PDF and a STEP file. Use that STEP for the hole coordinates. The drawing below is the outline and the window, so you can start the bezel before the board arrives.

How to print the pod

  1. Download the STEP from the Waveshare wiki page for the 7inch HDMI LCD (C) and drop it into the model as the thing the pocket has to hold.
  2. Cut the window to the lit area, 154.2 by 85.9 mm. A lip of the bezel can cover the dead border around that opening. It should not cover pixels.
  3. Add the 15 mm hood on the top edge. In the truck that edge is up, so the hood blocks the windshield.
  4. Leave a slot on the port edge for the HDMI plug and the micro-USB power plug, with a drip path so a spill on the dash does not run into the connector.
  5. Tilt the glass about 15° toward the driver. On a 2000 Tacoma the pod sits on the dash above the radio, bolted to the radio stack or the bezel screws. Keep fasteners out of both airbag covers.
  6. Print a test frame in draft first and sit the bare panel in it. Then print the pod. PETG or ASA handles a closed cab better than PLA.

The four big numbers are the clearance at each bumper corner. Green is beyond 36 inches, amber is 18 to 36, red is under 18. The traces along the bottom are the last few seconds of that same distance, so you can see a tree closing in before the number goes red.
